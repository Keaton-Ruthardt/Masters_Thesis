Making a "rail" out of a row of pads

A perfboard's holes are all separate. To make a shared power (3V3) or ground (GND) line, you join a whole row of pads into one connected strip — a rail — by soldering a bare wire across them. Here's exactly that, in three stages. Everything shown is the copper side (the bottom of the board).

STAGE 1 — WHAT YOU START WITH

Separate copper pads

Five copper pads, each with a hole in the middle. Right now they are not connected to each other — a wire in one hole does nothing to the next.

STAGE 2 — LAY THE WIRE

Rest a bare wire across the row

Strip a piece of 30 AWG so it's bare the whole length (or use a bare/solid wire), and lay it flat on top of the row of pads, touching each one. Don't solder yet — just rest it there.

STAGE 3 — SOLDER AT EACH PAD

Tack the wire to every pad → now it's one rail

Touch the iron to the wire and a pad at the same time, feed a bit of solder so a shiny blob bonds the wire to that pad. Do it at every pad in the row. The wire now electrically ties all five pads together — that whole row is one rail.

Do it twice

Make one row the 3V3 rail and another row the GND rail, each with its own bare wire soldered across it. Then a wire in any hole of the 3V3 row is connected to 3.3 V, and any hole of the GND row is connected to ground — that's how all four sensors share power and all four resistors share ground.

Where the sensor wires and A0–A3 solder

With the two rails made, here's the whole tiny hub. The 3V3 rail (top) and GND rail (bottom) are the rows you just built. Between them sit four node pads — one per sensor.

So each column is one sensor: its power wire solders onto the 3V3 rail; its signal wire and the matching A-pin wire (A0→S1, A1→S2, A2→S3, A3→S4) both solder into that column's node pad; and a 10 kΩ resistor bridges the node pad down to the GND rail. The sensor's male jumper pins just push through the holes and solder on the copper side like any other wire.

The key idea: the bare wire is the rail. Soldering it to each pad turns a row of separate holes into one shared power (or ground) line — so you don't run a separate wire from the XIAO to every sensor; they all tap the rail. Power wires meet the 3V3 rail, resistors meet the GND rail, and the A0–A3 wires meet the node pads.
