# Project Proposal: Instrumented Baseball for Grip Pressure Analysis

## A. Project Identification

This project proposes the design and construction of an instrumented baseball that measures grip pressure distribution at finger contact points during live pitching. The system embeds six to eight thin-film force-sensitive resistors along the ball's seams, a high-range inertial measurement unit for capturing spin rate and spin axis, a Bluetooth Low Energy microcontroller for wireless data transmission, and a Qi-compatible wireless charging subsystem. The complete electronics package targets a mass below fifteen grams, preserving regulation weight and balance. By pairing grip pressure data with ball-flight dynamics in a single self-contained instrument, the project creates a measurement platform that does not currently exist in the commercial or academic literature.

## B. Project Motivation

Modern pitch-tracking technologies have advanced rapidly in quantifying ball-flight metrics. Products such as PitchLogic embed inertial sensors inside a baseball to report velocity, spin rate, spin axis, and seam orientation in near-real-time. However, these systems measure only what the ball does after it leaves the hand; they capture no information about the grip forces that produced those outcomes. This gap matters because recent biomechanics research demonstrates a direct link between finger forces and pitch quality. A 2024 study published in MDPI Sensors showed that force development rate and the force differential between the index and middle fingers correlate with fastball spin rate and velocity. Despite this evidence, no existing tool provides pitchers or coaches with objective feedback on grip pressure distribution. Training adjustments remain guided by subjective feel, leaving a critical variable unmeasured.

## C. Project Objectives

The primary objective is to design and fabricate an instrumented baseball that captures real-time grip pressure distribution across all finger contact points during regulation pitching motions. The secondary objective is to fuse pressure data with concurrent IMU measurements so that each pitch produces a unified dataset linking grip force profiles to spin rate, spin axis, and release dynamics. A tertiary objective is to develop a companion data pipeline that visualizes and statistically analyzes these paired datasets, enabling pitchers and coaches to identify which grip adjustments produce measurable changes in pitch movement.

## 3. Intellectual Pursuit

The project presents a genuinely interdisciplinary engineering challenge. On the hardware side, it requires solving a constrained packaging problem: fitting a multi-sensor analog front end, microcontroller, lithium-polymer battery, and wireless charging coil inside a volume that must remain balanced about its center of mass and withstand repeated high-impact throws. Signal conditioning for the pressure array demands careful multiplexing and amplification to achieve sampling rates above one kilohertz across all channels. On the firmware side, the system must manage deterministic high-rate sampling, on-board buffering, and low-latency Bluetooth transmission within a power budget targeting fifty to one hundred pitches per charge. Beyond the embedded systems work, the project advances understanding of pitching biomechanics at the finger-ball interface, an area where laboratory studies exist but field-deployable instrumentation does not. Bridging that gap requires integrating knowledge from mechanical design, electrical engineering, signal processing, and sports science.

## 5. Bold Vision

The long-term vision is a future in which every pitcher, from youth leagues to professional organizations, can access objective data on how their grip shapes pitch movement. A mature version of this technology would allow a coach to show an athlete precisely which finger is applying too much or too little force and how a specific pressure adjustment shifts the spin axis by a quantifiable number of degrees. Beyond performance, grip pressure analytics could contribute to injury prevention by identifying force patterns associated with excessive forearm and elbow stress. The platform architecture is intentionally generalizable; the same sensor-and-IMU fusion approach could extend to cricket bowling, softball pitching, and football throwing, each a domain where grip is critical yet unmeasured. By converting grip from a subjective sensation into a structured dataset, this project lays the groundwork for a new category of athlete feedback tools grounded in engineering measurement rather than coaching intuition.
