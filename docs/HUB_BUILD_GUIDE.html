Building the sensor hub — two ways

The breadboard's job — joining one 3.3V pin to four sensors and returning four resistors to one ground — has to become permanent and tiny to fit the ball. Here's each method, step by step. You have heat-shrink and a soldering iron, which is all either one needs.

Breadboard vs. perfboard — they're different objects.

Breadboard — the white block with spring clips. Solderless, reusable, a rehearsal tool. Never cut it, never put it in the ball.

Perfboard (a.k.a. protoboard / stripboard) — a rigid board with a grid of copper-ringed holes you solder into. Permanent. About $0.50 a piece. This is what you cut small and embed. You are not cutting a breadboard — these are two different parts.

Point-to-point — no board at all; parts soldered directly to each other and insulated with heat-shrink. Use this if you don't have perfboard.

Method A · Perfboard hub

You're rebuilding the breadboard's two rails as two soldered rows, with the four resistors standing between them.

STEP 1

Cut a small piece of perfboard

Score deeply along one row of holes with a hobby knife (both sides), then snap it over a table edge. Aim for about 8 × 12 holes (~10 × 15 mm). Sand the rough edge. This is a perfboard, not your breadboard.

STEP 2

Assign your two rails

Decide the top row = 3.3V and the bottom row = GND. The middle rows hold the four ADC nodes. (If you happened to get stripboard, each row is already electrically connected — even easier.)

STEP 3

Solder the four resistors

Stand each 10 kΩ vertically: top leg = an ADC node, bottom leg into the GND rail. Space them one column apart. Bend the legs to hold them, solder, trim the excess. These are the parts you're waiting on — everything else you can pre-build.

STEP 4

Turn the two rows into rails

Lay a stripped bare wire along the top row and solder it to each hole — now every top hole is one connected 3.3V rail. Do the same on the bottom row for GND (it also catches every resistor's bottom leg). Skip this step if you used stripboard.

STEP 5

Wire the XIAO to the hub

3V3 → top rail (one wire). GND → bottom rail (one wire). Then A0, A1, A2, A3 each to one node (the top leg of a resistor). That's the "one pin feeds all four" made real — a single 3V3 wire lands on a rail the whole row shares.

STEP 6

Attach the four sensor harnesses & test

Each sensor's red wire → the 3.3V rail; its ADC wire → its node (same point as that resistor's top leg and the ADC pin wire). Then run the logger, press each pad, confirm the right channel moves — only then embed and pot.

Method B · Point-to-point

No board. You build the two rails as two wire junctions and let each resistor float in mid-air ("dead-bug"), insulating every joint with heat-shrink.

STEP 1

Build the 3.3V bus (one junction)

Gather the four sensor red wires + the XIAO 3V3 wire, twist them together, and solder into one joint. Slide a piece of heat-shrink over it and shrink. That single blob is your shared power rail.

STEP 2

Build the GND bus (second junction)

Same idea for ground: the XIAO GND wire plus the bottom leg of all four resistors meet in one soldered joint, heat-shrunk. (If four legs is too crowded, chain them: R1→R2→R3→R4→GND.)

STEP 3

Make one channel's node

Solder three things into one point: the sensor's ADC wire, the XIAO A0 wire, and the resistor's top leg. The resistor's other leg goes to the GND bus. Heat-shrink the node. That's one complete voltage divider, floating in mid-air.

STEP 4

Repeat for all four channels

Same node for S2→A1, S3→A2, S4→A3. When you're done you have two buses and four floating dividers — electrically identical to the perfboard version and to your breadboard, just smaller and hand-soldered.

STEP 5

Insulate, test, and bundle

Confirm no bare joints touch (a stray whisker between the 3.3V and GND buses is the same short that killed your PCB). Run the logger and press each pad. Then bundle the hub with the XIAO and LiPo into the ball's core, tails routed to the grip points.

Which should you pick? If you have (or can grab) a scrap of perfboard, Method A — it survives throwing far better. If not, Method B works with only what you have on hand. Both are the exact same circuit; the perfboard just holds it rigid instead of your solder joints doing all the work.
