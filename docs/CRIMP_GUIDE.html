SmartBall v2 · Crimping

Crimp the 3V3 bus

One female connector on the XIAO's 3V3 pin, feeding four wires out to the four sensors. Follow the pictures top to bottom.

1

Cut four red wires

Red = power, so you never mix it up. Long enough to reach each sensor with slack.

2

Strip ~4 mm off one end of each

Don't tin them. Crimps grip bare copper strands best — solder makes them work loose.

3

Twist all four bare ends into one bundle

Four thin 30 AWG wires twisted together ≈ one normal wire — that's what goes in the crimp.

4

Meet the crimp contact

The female contact has two sets of little wings: the wire crimp (small, grips bare copper) and the insulation crimp (big, grips the jacket). The round end plugs onto the pin.

5

Lay the bundle into the contact

Bare copper sits in the small wings; the insulation edge sits in the big wings. Copper should peek just past the wire wings — not into the socket.

6

Squeeze with the ratchet tool

Put it in the small die slot, squeeze until the ratchet releases. The wings fold down and bite the wire.

7

Slide it into a 1-pin housing

Push until it clicks and locks. The plastic housing is the insulation that stops channels touching later.

8

Plug onto the XIAO's 3V3 pin

One connector on 3V3, four red wires fanning out — each goes to a sensor's power pin.

9

Test every crimp before you trust it

Tug each wire (none should pull out) and beep from the contact to each wire end. A crimp that looks perfect can still be open.

!

Practice on scrap first. Crimp a few, cut them open or tug hard, until they hold every time. A bad crimp is the #1 way this build fails — and it always looks fine from the outside.

Same method builds the GND bus (four resistor legs → one contact → GND pin) and each node (sensor wire + resistor leg → one contact → analog pin). If four wires won't fit one barrel, twist them into a ferrule first, then crimp the ferrule.
