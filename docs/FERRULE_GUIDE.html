The ferrule method

Four wires will never fit one Dupont contact — the insulation clamp only grips a single jacket. So the wires get joined in a ferrule, and a jumper's factory-made contact is what plugs onto the pin. No soldering anywhere in the harness.

What a ferrule actually is

A soft metal tube with a plastic funnel on the back. You fill the tube with wire and squash it. The funnel guides strands in and insulates the tail — it is not part of the crimp.

!

Never tin the wires. Solder cold-flows under crimp pressure and the joint works loose months later. Bare stranded copper only — that's what the ferrule is for.

⌀

Go by the printed mm², not the colour. Ferrule colour codes come in two competing standards (French and German) — the same red can mean 1.0 mm² in one kit and 0.5 mm² in another. Read the size off the strip or the box.

1

Cut one end off a jumper wire

Keep one factory female contact — that's your pin connection, and it's better than anything crimped by hand. Cut the other end off and strip it 8 mm.

2

Heat-shrink — why, and when

The crimped barrel is bare metal at whatever voltage that joint carries. Six of them bundled in a 25 mm cavity is exactly how v1 died, so every ferrule gets covered.

Timing is relaxed here: all the wires enter the collar end, so the barrel's far tip has nothing coming out of it. Slip the tubing over the tip and slide it back — before or after crimping, either works.

3

Bundle and twist — one direction only

All conductors parallel, ends flush with each other, then twist the whole group tight like one wire. Loose strands are what refuse to enter the barrel.

!

Resistor legs are solid, not stranded. On the GND bus, cut all four legs to the same length and make sure each one bottoms out — solid wire slides back out of a ferrule far more easily than stranded while you're positioning the tool.

4

Insert until copper shows at the tip

Push the twisted bundle in through the funnel with a slight screwing motion. The barrel should be full — that's what makes a gas-tight crimp.

5

Position the tool on the barrel

Use the ferrule die — the square or hex jaw marked in mm² — not the Dupont die. The metal barrel sits inside the jaws; the plastic collar stays outside.

6

Read the crimp

A good ferrule crimp is visibly deformed — square or hex sided, not round. Round means it never compressed.

7

Wash, shrink, label

99% IPA over every joint, slide the heat-shrink up over the ferrule, then write the channel on the jacket while you still know which is which.

⟐

One channel, end to end

Every wire in the harness is a jumper with its factory contact kept. The only bare component is the resistor — top leg into the node ferrule, bottom leg into the GND ferrule. It never touches the XIAO.

!

Sleeve every resistor. Four bare resistors bundled in the centre cavity is the v1 failure relocated — if two top legs touch, you've shorted two analog channels together. Heat-shrink each one body-and-legs, bare tips only where they enter a ferrule.

All four channels at once

Every node ferrule sends its three conductors in three different directions: left to its sensor, right to its analog pin, down through the resistor to the shared ground.

Note what is not here: the sensors never touch GND. An A301 has two terminals — power and signal. Only the resistors reach ground.

!

Build one node completely, then beep it, before making the other three. If your 3-conductor technique is off you want to discover that once, not four times — and a node ferrule is the joint where a loose conductor is hardest to spot.

Exactly where the tubing goes

Three pieces per channel. The rule behind all of them: no bare metal may exist anywhere outside a ferrule barrel. Everywhere a sleeve stops and a barrel hasn't started is a gap — and a gap is a bridge.

Where all six land

Analog nodes on A0 · A1 · A2 · A3, identified by wire colour. Power and ground come off the opposite header. A4/A5 stay free — they're the I2C pins, worth keeping for a future IMU.

Flash firmware/isolation_test/isolation_test.ino

Hands off for 3 s while it zeroes the baselines, then press one pad at a time. Every other channel must stay under 5% of the pressed one. If a pair fails: a constant ratio across different press forces means an electrical bridge — find it. A ratio that varies with force is mechanical coupling through the ball — tolerable, but write it down.
